Petitions: The 2027 Reappointment Vote

You Don’t Get a Vote. They Do.

Judge Robert W. Rohm and Judge David E. Schwartz are associate judges. Whether they keep their seats after June 30, 2027 will be decided by secret ballot — cast not by the public, but by the circuit judges of DuPage County.

How an Associate Judge Keeps the Job

Most people assume the judges who decide their cases were chosen by voters. Associate judges were not. Under Illinois Supreme Court Rule 39, associate judges are appointed by the circuit judges of their own circuit, and the terms of every associate judge in the state expire together. The current terms end on June 30, 2027.

To stay on the bench, an associate judge must ask to be reappointed. Weeks before the terms expire, the Administrative Director of the Illinois Courts sends a ballot to every circuit judge in the circuit. Each circuit judge votes yes or no. The Director counts the ballots while “maintaining the secrecy of the ballots.” If three-fifths of the circuit judges voting say yes, the associate judge serves another term. If not, the seat is vacated.

No voter sees the ballot. No voter learns how any judge voted. The only people who decide whether Judge Rohm and Judge Schwartz remain on the bench are their colleagues — including the judges who denied Jennifer’s petitions to remove Judge Rohm from her case.

It Has Happened Before

Reappointment is not automatic. In 2023, the circuit judges of the 18th Judicial Circuit did not reappoint one of their own associate judges — one of only a handful of associate judges in the entire state who were not reappointed that year. When the circuit judges decide an associate judge should not continue, they have the power to say so. (Administrative Office of the Illinois Courts, June 27, 2023.)

What the Judges Have Conceded: Nothing

The judges who will cast these ballots are not hearing about this conduct for the first time. The Presiding Judge of the Law Division and the Chief Judge reviewed it on petition after petition. The Illinois Supreme Court had the entire record. Not one of them has conceded that a single act by any judge in this case was improper or unlawful.

On August 6, 2026, Judge Rohm vacated the January 22, 2020 order, which had taken away the most fundamental right Americans hold, the right to one’s own property, and had reduced Jennifer’s ownership of everything she owned to a license valid for one day and contingent on the words, actions and mindset of one friend on that day. He called it wrong, erroneous and a mistake. It had governed her case for 6.5 years. In the same hearing he announced: “And I find specifically that none of your rights have been violated by anybody, none.” A reader may decide that one ruling or another fell short of a legal standard. A record this long, reviewed by this many judges, without a single admission that anything was done improperly, shows a system built to control the outcome and protect its own. That is what the reappointment vote will test.

Why the Public Still Matters

The public cannot cast a ballot in this election. But the circuit judges who do vote are accountable to the public in a way associate judges are not. Every circuit judge in Illinois must stand for retention at the end of each term, and keeps the seat only if three-fifths of the voters say yes. The judges who will decide whether Judge Rohm and Judge Schwartz keep their seats will one day ask the voters of DuPage County to let them keep their own.

A secret ballot works best for the people casting it when no one is watching. The purpose of this page is to make sure that, this time, someone is. Every circuit judge who votes in 2027 will be able to say many things about that vote. Not one of them will be able to say they did not know what the record contains.

What Jennifer’s Law Will Do

Before the ballots are distributed in the spring of 2027, Jennifer’s Law will deliver a documented reappointment file on Judge Rohm and on Judge Schwartz to the circuit judges of the 18th Judicial Circuit. Each file will be built entirely from the public record — court orders, filed documents, and the official record of the proceedings — and each will be published here in full on the day it is delivered, so the public can read exactly what the judges were given.

What You Can Do

  • Sign the petitions. Do not reappoint Judge Rohm, do not reappoint Judge Schwartz, or sign against every judge on this site at once. The signatures are delivered to the circuit judges before the ballots go out, and the petitions never expire.
  • Read the record. Start with Judge Rohm, Judge Schwartz, the Illinois Supreme Court test, and the unlawful sanctions. Every order is published in full in Court Filings & Resulting Orders.
  • Share this page. Most people in DuPage County do not know that associate judges are never on a ballot. Tell them.
  • Get the reappointment files when they are published. Subscribe to the newsletter.
  • If you have appeared before Judge Rohm or Judge Schwartz, tell us what happened. Share your story. Accounts that can be supported by court records may be included in the reappointment files, with your permission.
  • Remember the names on your own ballot. The circuit judges who cast these votes stand for retention in DuPage County. Their records deserve the same attention.
Please Do Not Contact Judges About Pending Cases Do not write to, call, or approach any judge about Jennifer’s case or any other pending case. Communications to a judge about a pending matter are improper, and they can harm the very litigant they are meant to help. Reappointment and retention are questions of public accountability, and the place to raise them is in public.
